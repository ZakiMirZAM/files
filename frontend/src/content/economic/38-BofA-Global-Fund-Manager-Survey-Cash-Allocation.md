# Bank of America Global Fund Manager Survey (Cash Allocation)

## Part 1: Definition

The Bank of America Global Fund Manager Survey cash allocation measures the average percentage of their portfolios that professional fund managers are holding in cash rather than invested in stocks and other assets. It is expressed as a percentage and is released monthly based on a survey of fund managers who collectively manage a large pool of assets. The cash level reveals how cautious or confident professional investors are, since managers hold more cash when they are worried about markets and less cash when they are confident and fully invested. It is most useful as a contrarian signal at extremes.

The measure is constructed from a monthly survey that asks professional fund managers what share of their portfolios they are currently holding in cash. The cash percentage is included because it directly reveals how much money managers are keeping on the sidelines rather than investing, which reflects their level of caution or confidence. High cash levels are included as a meaningful signal because they show managers are defensive and holding back buying power, which means there is fuel available to push markets higher if conditions improve. Low cash levels are included as a signal because they show managers are fully invested and confident, which means there is little remaining buying power and the market is vulnerable if sentiment turns. The cash allocation is interpreted as a contrarian indicator because professional managers as a group tend to hold the most cash near market bottoms when fear is high and the least cash near market tops when confidence is high, so extremes in cash levels often precede reversals in the opposite direction.

## Part 2: Why this metric matters in finding the best sectors

The fund manager cash allocation matters for sector investing because it reveals the positioning and caution of professional investors, which at extremes serves as a contrarian signal for the overall risk environment and the rotation between aggressive and defensive sectors. When cash levels are high, professional managers are defensive and holding back buying power, which is a contrarian bullish signal because that cash can flow into markets and lift aggressive cyclical and growth sectors when confidence returns. When cash levels are low, managers are fully invested and confident, which is a contrarian bearish signal because there is little remaining buying power and the market is vulnerable to a pullback that would favor defensive sectors. The metric matters because professional positioning extremes have historically marked turning points, giving a contrarian edge for timing the rotation between risk-on and risk-off. It also reveals what the most informed investors are actually doing rather than just saying. The combination of a contrarian positioning signal and a read on professional caution makes the cash allocation a useful supplementary input for sector rotation timing.

## Ten Precise Qualitative Questions

1. Is the cash level at a genuine historical extreme that has reliably preceded market reversals, or merely elevated or low within its normal range where it provides less of a contrarian signal? The contrarian value of the cash allocation comes at true extremes, so a reading in the normal range should not drive a sector shift.

2. Are professional managers holding high cash because of genuine caution about the economic outlook, or because of recent market losses that forced defensive positioning? Caution-driven cash reflects a forward view, while loss-driven cash reflects a reaction to the past, which carries a different contrarian meaning.

3. Do the managers' cash levels match their stated views in the rest of the survey, or are they positioned differently from what they say, given that actual positioning carries more weight than stated opinions? When positioning and stated views align, the signal is more reliable than when managers say one thing but position differently.

4. Is the professional positioning confirmed by individual investor sentiment and market-priced fear, or is it an isolated reading that may not reflect the broader investor mood? Confirmation across positioning and sentiment measures strengthens the contrarian signal.

5. Is the high cash level occurring against genuine economic weakness that may justify the caution, or against a healthy economy where it is more likely a contrarian buying opportunity? Caution justified by real weakness is less of a contrarian signal than caution disconnected from a healthy economy.

6. What are the managers identifying as their biggest concerns in the survey, and are those concerns likely to materialize or to fade as overblown fears? The nature of the managers' concerns affects whether their caution is well-founded or an overreaction that presents a contrarian opportunity.

7. Are the managers crowded into the same trades and sectors, which would make those positions vulnerable to a sharp reversal if sentiment turns? Crowded professional positioning creates vulnerability to unwinding, which has specific implications for the crowded sectors.

8. Is the change in cash levels a gradual shift or a sharp move, since a sharp spike in cash after a decline often marks a more actionable bottom than a slow drift? The speed of the positioning change affects how reliably it marks a turning point.

9. How does the current cash level compare to past episodes, and does it match the levels seen at genuine market bottoms or tops? The cash level relative to historical turning points reveals whether the positioning is at an actionable extreme.

10. Is the positioning extreme reaching a level and persistence that warrants acting on the contrarian signal, or is it a single monthly reading that could reverse before a market turn materializes? Only a genuine and persistent extreme warrants a contrarian sector shift, since a single monthly reading can reverse.
