# US Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU)

## Part 1: Definition

The US Economic Policy Uncertainty index measures how much uncertainty about American economic policy is present in the daily news. It is built by Baker, Bloom, and Davis by measuring the share of newspaper articles that discuss the economy, policy, and uncertainty together, scaled into an index relative to its own history. The daily version updates every business day, making it one of the fastest available reads on the policy climate, far more timely than monthly or quarterly economic data. Higher readings mean greater uncertainty about the path of fiscal policy, monetary policy, taxes, regulation, and trade. Because it is drawn from what newspapers actually publish each day, it captures the policy mood as it unfolds rather than as it is later forecast or revised.

The index is constructed by counting the frequency of policy-uncertainty language in major US newspapers and normalizing it into a daily series. A rising level is interpreted as a deteriorating, less predictable policy climate, the kind that leads firms to delay investment and hiring and investors to demand more compensation for risk, because uncertainty about future rules and conditions makes commitments harder to plan. Spikes typically cluster around fiscal standoffs, debt-ceiling fights, contested elections, abrupt shifts in trade policy, and surprises in monetary policy. Because it is a daily series, it is well suited to flagging policy shocks in real time, though its day-to-day noise means the trend matters more than any single reading.

## Part 2: Why this metric matters in finding the best sectors

The US EPU matters for sector investing because domestic policy uncertainty shapes the willingness of American businesses and consumers to commit capital, which determines whether risk-on cyclical sectors or defensive, stable-demand sectors are favored. When US policy uncertainty is low, the domestic climate is predictable and supports a risk-on environment in which cyclical and growth sectors can lead, because firms invest and households spend with confidence. When US policy uncertainty is high, the climate is unpredictable and pressures domestically focused cyclicals first, because business investment, hiring, and consumer big-ticket spending are most sensitive to unstable policy, while defensive sectors with non-discretionary demand hold up better. The metric matters with particular force for domestically oriented cyclical sectors such as Financials, Consumer Discretionary, and Industrials, whose fortunes track US business and consumer confidence closely, and it provides a fast early-warning signal because of its daily frequency. Because it is timely, it can flag shifts in the policy climate before slower economic data confirm them.

## Part 5: Metrics to analyze alongside it

| Paired metric | Why this metric should be read alongside the US EPU | How combining the two metrics sharpens the answer to which sector is the best investment |
|---|---|---|
| Global Economic Policy Uncertainty | The global index puts the US reading in worldwide context, so reading them together shows whether US uncertainty is part of a broad global episode or a domestic-only phenomenon. | When both rise together, the uncertainty is broad and both domestic and globally exposed cyclicals face pressure, favoring defensives. When the US index rises while the global index is calm, the issue is domestic, which points to US-focused cyclicals as the main risk and may leave globally diversified sectors more insulated. |
| VIX | The EPU measures the policy-uncertainty climate while the VIX measures current market fear, so reading them together shows whether elevated policy uncertainty has been priced into volatility yet. | When the US EPU is high but the VIX is low, policy risk is building during market complacency, a setup that favors adding defensives before volatility rises. When both are elevated, fear and policy uncertainty reinforce each other, strengthening the case for defensive positioning. |
| 10-year, 2-year, 3-month rate | Policy uncertainty often centers on fiscal and monetary policy, which move interest rates, so reading rates alongside the EPU shows whether uncertainty is translating into rate volatility that pressures rate-sensitive sectors. | When high EPU coincides with rising or volatile rates, the most rate-sensitive sectors such as Real Estate and Utilities face added pressure on top of the uncertainty. When high EPU coincides with falling rates from a flight to safety, defensive bond-substitute sectors can benefit even as cyclicals struggle. |

## Ten Precise Qualitative Questions

1. Is the spike in the daily index a short-lived reaction to a single headline, or the start of a sustained rise that warrants repositioning, given how noisy the daily series is?

2. Is the uncertainty about a specific, resolvable event such as a debt-ceiling deadline or an election, or about an open-ended structural issue that could keep the index elevated?

3. Is the policy uncertainty centered on fiscal policy, monetary policy, taxes, regulation, or trade, since each affects different sectors with different force?

4. Has the elevated reading begun to show up in real activity such as softer business investment, hiring, or consumer spending, or is it still confined to news coverage?

5. Is the US index elevated alongside the global index, indicating a broad episode, or is it rising on its own as a purely domestic phenomenon?

6. How does the current level compare with past US policy shocks, and did those episodes precede meaningful weakness in domestically focused cyclical sectors?

7. Are cyclical sectors already discounting the uncertainty in their valuations, or is the risk not yet reflected in prices?

8. Is the uncertainty being accompanied by a flight to safety in Treasuries that would compound the pressure on risk-on sectors while supporting defensives?

9. Does the timing of the uncertainty coincide with a fragile point in the business cycle, which would amplify its effect on confidence-sensitive sectors?

10. Is the policy uncertainty likely to resolve in a direction that favors particular sectors, such as fiscal stimulus benefiting cyclicals or tighter regulation pressuring a specific industry?
