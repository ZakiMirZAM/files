# SOFR-OIS Spread

## Part 1: Definition

The SOFR-OIS Spread measures the gap between two short-term interest rates that reveals stress in the banking system's funding markets. SOFR is a rate based on the cost of borrowing cash overnight using government securities as collateral, and OIS is a rate that reflects the market's expectation of the central bank's policy rate over a period. The spread between them is expressed in basis points, and it updates daily. When the spread is narrow, bank funding markets are calm, and when it widens, it signals that banks are finding it more expensive or harder to access short-term funding, a sign of financial stress.

The spread is constructed by subtracting the policy-expectation rate from the secured funding rate. The secured overnight funding rate is included because it reflects the actual cost banks and financial institutions pay to borrow cash for a very short term against safe collateral, capturing the real-time conditions in the most important short-term funding market. The overnight index swap rate is included because it reflects the market's expectation of the average central bank policy rate over the same period, which serves as the baseline risk-free cost of money without funding stress. Subtracting the policy-expectation rate from the actual funding rate isolates the extra cost that reflects funding pressure rather than expected policy, which is included as the key signal because it reveals whether the funding markets are functioning smoothly or under strain. The spread is included as an indicator because short-term funding markets are the plumbing of the financial system, and when banks struggle to fund themselves, stress can spread rapidly through the system, making the spread an early warning of financial strain that often appears before broader market stress.

## Part 2: Why this metric matters in finding the best sectors

The SOFR-OIS Spread matters for sector investing because it is an early warning gauge of stress in the banking system's funding markets, which signals financial strain that affects Financials directly and the broader market through the risk environment. When the spread is narrow, bank funding markets are calm and the financial system is functioning normally, which supports a risk-on environment favorable to cyclical and risk-sensitive sectors. When the spread widens, banks are finding short-term funding more expensive or scarce, which signals financial stress that hurts Financials directly and often precedes broader market turmoil that favors defensive positioning. The metric matters with particular force for Financials because bank funding costs and access directly affect their profitability and stability, so a widening spread is a direct warning for the sector. Because funding stress can spread rapidly through the interconnected financial system, the spread serves as an early signal that lets investors rotate defensive before stress reaches the broader market. The combination of a direct link to Financials and an early warning of systemic stress makes the spread a focused but valuable input during periods of financial strain.

## Ten Precise Qualitative Questions

1. Is the widening in the spread being driven by genuine funding stress where banks struggle to access cash, or by temporary technical factors such as quarter-end or year-end balance sheet adjustments that routinely widen the spread briefly? Technical widening reverses quickly and does not signal real stress, while genuine funding stress warrants a defensive response.

2. Is the funding stress concentrated in specific institutions that may be facing their own problems, or is it broad-based across the banking system in a way that signals systemic strain? Institution-specific stress affects those firms, while broad-based stress signals a systemic problem that threatens the whole financial sector.

3. Is the central bank responding to the funding stress by providing liquidity through its facilities, and is that response succeeding in calming the funding markets? When the central bank intervenes effectively, funding stress can be contained, while a stress that persists despite intervention signals a more serious problem.

4. Is the funding stress appearing alongside stress in other short-term funding markets such as commercial paper or repo markets, which would confirm broad-based funding strain? Confirmation across funding markets strengthens the signal that the financial plumbing is under genuine strain.

5. Are foreign banks that rely on dollar funding showing particular stress, which can transmit through the global financial system and signal broader dollar funding shortages? Dollar funding stress among foreign banks can spread globally, making it a sign of wider systemic strain.

6. Is the spread widening before stress appears in equity markets and credit spreads, which would make it an early warning that lets investors position defensively ahead of broader turmoil? Because funding stress often precedes broader market stress, an early widening provides a valuable lead for defensive rotation.

7. How does the current spread level compare to its history, and is it at levels associated with normal conditions, mild strain, or crisis? The spread level relative to history reveals whether the funding stress is routine or approaching crisis levels that demand defensive positioning.

8. Is the funding stress being caused by a specific shock such as a bank failure or a market disruption, or by a gradual buildup of caution as conditions tighten? A shock-driven spike carries different implications than a gradual widening, affecting how to respond.

9. Is the spread consistent with what other measures of financial stress such as credit spreads and the volatility index are showing, or is it diverging in a way that provides an early or isolated signal? When the spread leads other stress measures, it provides an early warning, while confirmation across measures signals broad stress.

10. Is the widening large enough and sustained enough to signal genuine financial stress that warrants a defensive rotation, or is it a brief technical move that will reverse without consequence? Only a meaningful and sustained widening warrants defensive positioning, since the spread routinely widens briefly for technical reasons that do not reflect real stress.
