# Sector Rotation weights

Each weight sets how much one measure counts toward the score. A higher weight gives that measure more say. Type a new weight from 0 to 5. A weight of 0 keeps the row visible for reference without letting it move the score. The table totals update on your device right away.

Fill from data sets every row with a data feed to the latest economic reading and leaves the rest unchanged. Treat it as a starting point. This page teaches you to read the economy, so change any reading where your own view differs.

## Why inflation and commodity rows count for less

A rising inflation print looks like good news for Basic Materials, Energy, and Industrials, but the benefit is only real when the trend is durable. Most inflation spikes fade within a year, and a temporary spike does not raise the multi-year earnings power that share prices are built on. Fill from data therefore tests every rising PPI or CPI reading two ways before it counts: the rise must also hold over a full 12 months, and the 5-year breakeven must be rising too. Breakevens are the market's own forward price of inflation, so realized inflation against flat breakevens is a spike the market has already judged transitory. A reading that fails either test is set to stable and scores nothing.

The individual commodity price rows (oil, copper, iron ore, wheat, and the import price index) default to weight 0. A commodity's price level does not directly drive sector stock returns: it drives the revenue of companies that sell that commodity, and the market prices those swings quickly. The rows stay in the table because reading them is still part of reading the economy.

## Pricing power is the real lesson

A company whose profit is set by a commodity price it cannot control is a price taker. When the commodity falls, the profit goes with it, and no reading in this matrix changes that. A company with pricing power passes cost increases through to customers and does not need an inflation reading to protect its margin. So even when this matrix favors a commodity sector, the durable investment inside that sector is the company with pricing power, not the one that merely rides the commodity. If a business is heavily based off the price of a commodity and has no pricing power, a favorable reading here is not a reason to buy it.
