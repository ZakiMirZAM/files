# Stage 4: compare companies

This stage puts the main companies of one industry side by side. Open it from the Comparative item in the left menu, or from an industry page with the "Compare the companies in this industry" button. The companies then appear as a table.

The table is a grid. Each row is one company, shown by its stock symbol, which is the short code that identifies it, for example AAPL for Apple. Each column is one measure, such as a profit number or a debt number. Related columns sit together under grouped headings.

## How to read the colors

Each cell is tinted to rank the companies down that column. Green means the company looks better on that one measure and red means it looks worse. So read down a column to see who leads and who lags on a single measure, and read across a row to see the whole profile of one company. A company that is green down most of a column group is the one worth opening first.

## What you click

Two tables appear. The first is a value ranking and the second is a company scorecard. Above the ranking sit two filters: a toggle that limits the list to United States and developed-market companies, and a box where you type a smallest company size in billions of dollars. Use them to cut a long list down to names you can actually work through.

Click a company symbol to open its full report, which is Stage 5. Click any number cell to open the side panel, which draws that company's history for that measure. The question this page answers: which companies here look best on the numbers.
