# Stage 1: the economy

The Economic page is the first step. It shows broad measures of the whole economy and the whole stock market, such as interest rates and how expensive shares look against company profits. An interest rate is the fee charged to borrow money. When the market looks expensive, it means today's share prices are high compared with the profits behind them.

The question this stage answers is simple. Are conditions helping companies right now, or making things harder for them. Slow growth or rising interest rates make it harder for companies to grow, and that changes how generous you should be with any value estimate later.

## What you see and click

Near the top of the page are two panels. The Fed model panel sets company earnings against the return on safe government bonds, which is one way of asking whether shares or bonds look better value today. The S&P 500 valuation panel estimates a fair value for the market as a whole. The S&P 500 is an index of 500 large US companies.

Below those is a list called Metrics. Each measure is one row. Click a row and it opens to show a chart of that measure over time and its full definition in plain words. Click the row again to close it. Start with two or three rows rather than all of them. Some rows say "chart data source pending", which means the definition is there but the chart is not yet wired up.

The shaded bands on the charts mark past US recessions, the periods when the economy shrank. They are there so you can see how each measure behaved when things went badly, which is usually more revealing than how it behaves in good times.
