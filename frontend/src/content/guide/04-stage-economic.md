# Stage 1: the economy

The Economic page is the first step. The page shows broad measures of the whole economy and the whole stock market. Examples are interest rates and how expensive the market looks. An interest rate is the fee charged to borrow money. "How expensive the market looks" compares today's share prices to company earnings, so a high reading means prices are high relative to profits.

The question this stage answers is simple. Is the wider economy helping companies right now, or is it making things harder for them. Conditions like slow growth or rising interest rates can make it harder for companies to grow.

## What you see and click

Near the top, the page shows a Fed model panel and an S&P 500 valuation panel. The S&P 500 is an index of 500 large US companies. Below those is a list called Metrics. Each measure is one row.

Click a row to open it. The row expands to show a chart of that measure over time, plus its full plain-language definition. Click the row again to close it. Some rows say "chart data source pending". The definition still applies; only the chart is missing.

Shaded bands on the charts mark past US recessions. A recession is a period when the economy shrinks. The bands let you see how each measure behaved during hard times.
